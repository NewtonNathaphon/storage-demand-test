## The business in one sentence

Build a demand-led storage operating network: find where people need storage first, create/control storage supply where demand is proven, and eventually scale using landlords' property and capital rather than funding every location ourselves.

The website is the demand engine and data layer for the physical storage business. Physical storage is where the strongest initial economics are expected; the later management/network layer could enable expansion without personally funding each site.

Working brand: SpaceBuddy, subject to domain and brand checks. Parking may eventually fit the brand but is not part of the current storage MVP.

## Commercial rationale and supplied precedents

The supplied thesis cites:
- Colliers' May 2026 Bangkok self-storage research: improving occupancy, with suburban Bangkok the strongest-performing submarket, supported by households and SMEs.
- Australia's Spacer: more than 30,000 spaces and positioning as an “Expedia for space,” combining unused local space with commercial storage supply.
- Extra Space Storage: 1,964 third-party-owned facilities under management, alongside owned and JV facilities.

No source URLs or underlying reports were supplied with this message. Counts, dates, definitions and the Colliers interpretation must be checked against primary sources before reliance. International examples illustrate mechanisms; they do not prove Thai demand, local margins or Newton's execution advantage.

The hypothesis is that Thailand has fragmented storage/property supply that a smaller, agile operator can serve using demand-led site selection and professional operation.

## Flywheel

1. Website, Google and content capture where customers need storage, size, duration and willingness to pay.
2. Identify genuine qualified-demand clusters.
3. Find/control a suitable property in the cluster and underwrite its economics.
4. Convert and operate storage.
5. Use the central demand engine to fill it.
6. Record actual occupancy, realized pricing, acquisition cost, customer duration, capital requirements, labour and other operating costs.
7. Use measured results to choose the next site and test replication.
8. With operating proof, approach landlords: they bring property/capital; the company brings demand, feasibility, design and operation.
9. Expand with progressively less Newton capital where contracts and economics justify it; accumulate demand, operating data, relationships and brand.

Tha Phra remains Candidate Site A. A low asking rent is a research lead, not proof it should become Node #1. Measure its physical potential, then compare it against demand and underwriting elsewhere.

## Illustrative node economics — not underwriting

Required real inputs: rentable sqm, achieved price, physical/economic occupancy, customer acquisition cost, capital expenditure, replacement labour and other operating expenses.

The supplied example assumes:
- Gross building area: 200 sqm.
- Rentable area after conversion: approximately 140 sqm.
- Realized price: THB 800/sqm/month.
- Full physical revenue capacity: 140 × 800 = THB 112,000/month.
- At 80% occupancy: THB 89,600/month, rounded to THB 90,000 in the discussion.
- Rent, utilities, security, software, cleaning, maintenance, marketing and replacement labour: approximately THB 40,000–50,000/month.
- Result under those assumptions: about THB 39,600–49,600/month operating surplus, discussed as THB 40,000–50,000.

The wider supplied scenarios were THB 60,000–80,000+ per month with better pricing/occupancy, or THB 10,000–20,000 with weaker economics. These wider ranges have not been supported by a complete cost model. Do not label this surplus net profit, free cash flow or sustainable EBITDA without defining treatment of all costs, central overhead, capex, depreciation, financing, taxes, vacancy/discounts and reserves.

Do not invest simply because rent is THB 20,000/month. Demand evidence and full property underwriting must earn the assumptions.

This is multiplication of hypothetical inputs, not evidence that such EBITDA or multiples will be achieved or apply to this company. ## Expected defensibility — hypotheses

- Physical locality: capacity in one catchment cannot be replaced by shipping cheap space from elsewhere.
- Recurring use: customers may rent for months; actual duration and retention must be observed.
- Fragmented underused commercial property could provide opportunities outside large institutional operators' preferred formats.
- Technology/AI could reduce small-team workload through photo sizing support, structured enquiries, preliminary property assessment, routine service and site-selection analysis.
- The intended accumulated advantage is demand/operating data, local relationships, property control and operating contracts, not exclusive access to an AI model.
- A landlord-funded route could reduce capital intensity only after proof and commercially sound agreements; it is not automatic or free expansion.

The business is not literally future-proof. These defenses must be demonstrated in practice.

## Four failure conditions

1. Demand: customers do not pay enough in viable catchments.
2. Property economics: appropriate properties cost too much relative to achievable storage revenue.
3. Labour: small sites need too much human supervision or Newton's unpaid presence.
4. Acquisition: renters cost too much to acquire and replenish.

If these do not work, the platform/AI narrative cannot rescue the economics. Change or stop the thesis promptly rather than use the scale story to justify weak sites. No numerical go/no-go thresholds have been agreed yet; establish them during underwriting instead of inventing them in the app.

## Positioning and scale of sites

Demand-led distributed storage operator. Not merely a cheaper incumbent, an app, or a spare-bedroom marketplace.

Small/fragmented commercial properties are an initial experimental wedge, not a permanent size restriction. The supplied examples of 150–250 sqm, a possible 350–600 sqm economic sweet spot, or a future 1,200 sqm JV are illustrative options, not validated size recommendations or inventory.

Micro is the wedge. Demand-led storage operation is the business.

## Time-horizon aspirations, not commitments

- Year 1: prove demand, physical space, capital and labour economics with a real node. The supplied aspirational contribution range is THB 50,000–150,000/month if things go well; this is not an output proven by the single-node example.
- Year 3: prove repeatability, centralized acquisition and a genuinely landlord-funded/JV location. Supplied aspiration: THB 200,000–500,000/month.
- Year 10: a network across selected Thai markets, possibly dozens of controlled/managed sites, lean operations and landlords bringing suitable properties. Supplied aspiration: THB 500,000–1 million+ per month, potentially more if management economics scale.

These are optional success scenarios rather than delivery deadlines, growth forecasts or guaranteed income. Later choices might include continuing cash generation, expansion, investors or a strategic sale.

## Evidence gates and immediate mission

Use demand to tell us where to control storage. Use successful storage to prove operation. Use proof to earn access to other people's property and capital.

Gate 1: establish where Node #1 should exist and whether it can produce strong recurring profit without Newton working there every day.

Gate 2: demonstrate replication at Node #2 rather than extrapolating from one good site.

Gate 3: demonstrate that landlords will actually enter viable funded management/JV arrangements.

Push investment and expansion as evidence clears these gates, not before. Today the website is market discovery, Tha Phra is supply research, and the software scope remains the narrow P0/P1/P2 MVP in PROJECT_HANDOFF.md.
