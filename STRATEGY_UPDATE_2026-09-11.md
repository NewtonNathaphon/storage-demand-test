## Brand and customer promise

StorageBuddy is a professionally managed, value-oriented self-storage brand. Give customers a facility that feels clean, safe, reliable and properly run without charging a premium-brand price. The personality is modern, helpful and easy to deal with: Buddy.

Current brand concept, with Thai diacritics restored from the supplied paste:

Customer-facing promise: **professional care, thoughtful help, and a Buddy price.** Broader positioning: **professional quality at a sensible Buddy price.**

Helping customers find the right size remains an important service feature that prevents over-renting. It is **not the moat or the entire brand proposition**. Earlier “right space” language remains useful supporting messaging; it no longer defines the whole positioning. This update does not itself rename the brand or authorize replacing website artwork/copy.

- Whole-building monthly rent scenarios remain THB 100,000 / 150,000 / 200,000. Low and mid cases are considered more attractive; profitability still needs underwriting.

## Physical product and room mix

Build simple rooms within a polished, professionally maintained environment. Prioritize bright lighting, clean durable floors, ventilation, humidity control/monitoring, CCTV, controlled building access, pest control, carts, lift/loading convenience, clear signage and strong housekeeping.

Individual rooms can use modular galvanized partitions, simple metal doors, overhead security mesh to control room height and prevent climbing, and customer-owned or inexpensive physical padlocks. Use electronic access **at building level**. Do not install electronic locks on every room unless later demand justifies them. AC/climate-controlled space should be a limited premium product rather than the whole-building default.

Keep Phase 1 small-room-heavy without locking the mix prematurely: **2–5 sqm** as a major category, some approximately 1 sqm/locker space, some **5–8 sqm** rooms, and limited larger rooms for SMEs/families.

Consider low-cost demountable partitions for part of the facility: fixed corridors, standardized panels, reusable side walls and selected rooms that can split or combine in response to occupancy data. Keep this simple. If flexibility costs the equivalent of **2–3 normal partitions**, it is not worthwhile under the supplied direction. The phased six-floor rollout already supplies most of the flexibility.

## Pricing

Be **sharp, not cheap**. Aim to feel approximately **5–15% better value** than professional competitors while preserving margin and perceived quality. This is a value-positioning target, not an automatic across-the-board discount or an approved tariff.

- Monthly rent is the normal price.
- Longer prepaid terms may earn modest discounts.
- Promote weak sizes selectively.
- Remove promotions or consider price increases for nearly full sizes.
- Optimize **realized revenue per rentable sqm**, not occupancy at any price.

Buddy price means a surprisingly good facility for the money, not Bangkok's cheapest storage. Keep list price, realized rent and vacant capacity distinct when measuring performance.

## Marketing and website

Customer acquisition is the main offensive tool; avoid a price war. Priorities are Google Search, Google Maps, local SEO, Meta use-case ads, reviews and retargeting/referrals.

The website serves as salesperson and demand sensor: understand what customers will store, recommend a likely size, show transparent pricing/availability, capture contact details, and route to LINE/reservation. This is the intended journey, not a claim that live pricing, inventory and reservations already exist.

Track customer location, requested size, recommended size, budget, move-in date, expected duration, source, quoted price, conversion and actual rented size. Distinguish recommendations from actual rentals.

Proposed initial marketing spend is approximately **THB 30,000–50,000/month**, scaling only when CAC and conversion are proven. Saving this proposal does not authorize ad spending. Be aggressive in acquiring customers, not discounting.

## Lean, owner-light operations

Use building-level electronic access, CCTV, automated reminders, clear contracts, simple billing, PromptPay/card/bank transfer and a clear delinquency process. These are intended operating capabilities, not confirmation of current readiness.

Individual room keys are customers' responsibility. A business customer can rent several rooms under one company account and assign keys internally. No need for smart room locks or complex per-room user permissions.

Allow retrieval and light organizing. Do not become fulfillment, warehousing services, moving or logistics. Refer moving to partners. Aim for minimal staffing, outsourced cleaning/security where practical and centralized customer support.

## Rollout and expansion gate

Prove demand and economics → build Phase 1 → collect actual occupancy, pricing and CAC data → reconfigure where useful → expand the next floor only when the current phase is consistently strong.

A reasonable proposed expansion trigger combines:

- Sustained occupancy around **75% or higher**.
- Positive site contribution.
- Acceptable CAC and good retention.
- Clear evidence of shortages in relevant sizes.

This is a combined gate, not permission to expand solely on reaching 75%. The required observation period and financial thresholds remain to be defined.

After Rama 3 proves the model, StorageBuddy could operate other underutilized buildings: landlords/property owners fund property and fit-out capital; StorageBuddy provides design, brand, marketing and operations. Prove the first facility before pursuing that expansion model.

## Real moat

**Favorable property economics + disciplined facility CAPEX + strong local customer acquisition + pricing/inventory intelligence + operational consistency + trusted brand reputation.**

The size calculator, partitions and logo are not the moat. The underlying business is a disciplined recurring-income storage operator.
